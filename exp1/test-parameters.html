Standard Testing Procedure

  1. Collect a fresh, midstream urine specimen in a sterile collection container.
  2. Remove a test strip from the container and recap the bottle immediately.
  3. Immerse all reagent pads of the strip completely into the urine sample for exactly one second.
  4. Draw the edge of the strip against the rim of the container to remove excess urine.
  5. Blot the long edge of the strip gently on an absorbent paper towel to prevent cross-contamination between reagent pads.
  6. Place the strip onto the reader loading tray and initiate the optical scan.
  7. Review the digitized results on the screen and export the verified diagnostic report.

Healthcare Stakeholders & Roles

  • Doctor
    • Reviews automated urinalysis test results
    • Correlates findings with patient clinical symptoms
    • Prescribes treatment plans and medication
  • Laboratory Technician
    • Calibrates the optical reader using standard calibration strips
    • Prepares and processes patient specimens
    • Verifies test values and manages quality control
  • Patient
    • Provides fresh midstream urine sample following hygiene instructions
    • Receives and reviews final diagnostic reports
    • Follows medical recommendations given by the clinician
  • Hospital Administrator
    • Manages system access credentials and security roles
    • Monitors equipment maintenance schedules and reagent inventories
    • Ensures compliance with healthcare regulatory standards

Medical Abbreviations Description List

UTI
Urinary Tract Infection — An infection in any part of the urinary system, commonly diagnosed through positive leukocyte and nitrite readings.
RBC
Red Blood Cells — Indicates intact erythrocytes or hemoglobin in urine, which may signify renal stones, trauma, or glomerulonephritis.
WBC
White Blood Cells — Indicates leukocytes in urine, suggesting immune response to an infection or inflammatory process.
pH
Potential of Hydrogen — A measure of urine acidity or alkalinity, typically between 4.5 and 8.0, influenced by diet and renal tubular function.
SG
Specific Gravity — Reflects the kidney's ability to concentrate urine by measuring the ratio of urine density compared to water.
GLU
Glucose — Measures sugar concentration in urine; glucosuria typically indicates elevated blood glucose exceeding the renal threshold.
PRO
Protein — Measures urinary albumin excretion, where elevated levels suggest glomerular barrier impairment.
KET
Ketones — Byproducts of fat metabolism, often elevated during diabetic ketoacidosis, fasting, or intense exercise.
BIL
Bilirubin — A breakdown product of hemoglobin; presence in urine indicates biliary obstruction or hepatic disease.
URO
Urobilinogen — Conjugated bilirubin transformed by intestinal bacteria, useful in differentiating hemolytic jaundice from liver disease.
